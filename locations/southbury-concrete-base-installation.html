Concrete Base Work Planned Around Your Southbury Property

We set out the footprint against the building supplier’s requirements and check its position against fences, nearby paving and drainage. The finished height needs to work with the surrounding garden without directing surface water towards the building.

Turf, topsoil and unsuitable material are removed before the sub-base goes in. Where excavation exposes soft ground, we assess the support needed rather than covering it and carrying on.

We compact the sub-base in layers, secure the formwork and check the dimensions before pouring. Concrete thickness and any reinforcement are specified for the intended load and site conditions.

Narrow side paths affect how spoil comes out and materials go in. We agree a suitable carrying or pumping arrangement and keep delivery movements organised so neighbouring entrances remain accessible.

Why Getting The Base Right First Matters

A smooth surface only tells you how the concrete was finished. We check the ground beneath it and the building requirements before deciding how the base should be constructed.

Around occupied family homes, we agree where materials will sit and how the work route will be kept separate from everyday garden use. Excavations and fresh concrete need to remain clear of children and pets.

Before leaving, we explain how to protect the concrete and when loading can begin. Your building installation should be booked around the agreed curing period, rather than assuming the base is ready the following morning.

Avoid Base Movement Beneath Your Garden Building

Concrete poured over soft spots or poorly compacted fill can settle unevenly. That movement can leave the building out of level, strain connections and cause doors to bind.

We deal with unsuitable ground before the pour and check any existing base before considering reuse. Adding fresh concrete over a moving slab does not resolve the failed support underneath.

The footprint also needs checking against the supplier’s drawing. An oversized exposed edge can collect rainwater beside the building, while an undersized base can leave parts unsupported.
