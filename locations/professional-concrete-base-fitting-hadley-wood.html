Concrete Base Installation In Hadley Wood

Our concrete base team installs bases for homeowners in Hadley Wood, preparing the ground to suit the building going on top.

A detached home can have a generous rear garden and still leave little room to move materials down the side. We check that route alongside the proposed base position before arranging excavation and concrete delivery.

Our concrete base installation service covers setting out, excavation, sub-base preparation and the finished pour. You can discuss the groundwork before committing to a building delivery date.

A Base Fitted To Your Building And Garden

We work from your building supplier’s base requirements, checking the footprint, loading and required tolerances. Slab thickness and any reinforcement are specified for the job rather than treated as a standard detail.

Finished height matters where the base meets an existing path or patio. We agree the level and surrounding drainage so surface water is directed away from the building.

Where excavation exposes soft ground, we address the weak material before placing the compacted sub-base. Deeper unstable ground or significant tree influence may need a separate foundation design before work proceeds.

How We Plan And Fit Your Concrete Base

We start with the dimensions, garden photographs and access width, then arrange a site assessment where needed. We confirm the working area, spoil removal and delivery method before booking the pour.

The footprint is marked out and excavated, with buried services checked before digging. We prepare and compact the sub-base in layers, then set the formwork to the agreed dimensions and levels.

Any specified membrane and reinforcement are fitted before the concrete is placed and finished. We explain curing protection and agree when the building can be installed; a finished surface does not mean the slab is ready for loading.

Working Around Hadley Wood Homes And Narrow Access

Narrow side paths can limit machinery and wheelbarrow access, even at detached properties. Gate openings, tight turns and the distance from the road determine how we move excavated soil out and concrete in.

We agree protection for existing paving and keep materials within a defined working area. Around occupied family homes, delivery timing and a safe route past the work need settling before installation starts.

Mature planting also affects where digging and material storage can take place. We check nearby trees and established borders rather than assuming every clear patch of lawn is suitable for excavation.

Uneven support beneath a base can allow movement that distorts the building above. Checking the ground and building requirements at the start reduces the risk of having to lift the structure and replace the base later.
